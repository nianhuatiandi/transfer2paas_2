Badminton annotation prompt v0.7 — staged pipeline

Goal: describe the complete supplied clip, readable text, audible speech, and atomic sports actions. Write descriptions in English and preserve original-language speech/text. Use evidence accuracy before completeness of labels or richness of prose. Missing evidence remains uncertain, not a guessed positive or negative event.

Pipeline contract:
1. Local preprocessing reads source duration/fps, detects candidate scene cuts and extracts actual timestamped frames at approximately 10 fps, including the first and last frame of every shot. Cut detections are segmentation aids, not semantic labels.
2. Analyse each shot independently using its frames. The visual-stage JSON task is supplied separately. Track shot-scoped person IDs and every person's changes. A new shot does not establish a new camera end or identity continuity.
3. Transcribe full-clip audio separately, without visual descriptions or sport-specific vocabulary that could bias recognition. The audio-stage task is supplied separately.
4. The program merges chronological observations, assigns action IDs after sorting by start time, checks shot bounds, actor references and labels, and renders these exact headings: Storyline; Visible Text; Speakers and Transcript; Atomic Sports Actions. Per-stage responses are JSON, not the final report.

Evidence rules for the visual stage:
- Frame timestamps are actual source-frame times. Their spacing bounds temporal certainty. Describe observed transitions; mark an onset/offset cropped if absent. Never invent intermediate poses or outgoing shuttle paths.
- Name a technique when its distinguishing evidence is visible; use fallback labels when it is not. Do not require an eventual landing or a perfectly captured contact frame if the visible sequence otherwise supports the stroke. Conversely, a raised racket or sound alone does not establish a stroke subtype.
- Footwork, racket movement and shuttle travel are independent observations. A lunge can overlap a lift. Record repeated attempts separately. Named recovery footwork is one event with recovery stated as its purpose, not a duplicate recovery event.
- Use the exact canonical English label below. The Chinese names are explanatory mappings. No target action count or required technique is prescribed. Example sequences and titles must not create events.
- Recognize forehand/backhand from the actor's visible striking mechanics, never from screen-left/right alone. Describe body travel and shuttle trajectory using distinct screen directions.
- Speech cannot be inferred from captions or mouth movement. Text must be readable; do not complete logos or captions from familiarity. Conversational racket gestures stay in storyline, not Atomic Sports Actions.
- Deception, attacking/defensive intent, spin, and opponent response require their own evidence. Describe an observed preparation change without guessing intent or success.

Canonical vocabulary (terminology informed by BWF Shuttle Time; annotation thresholds are task-specific):
Footwork labels
- split step | 启动步／分腿垫步: A brief preparatory unweighting or small hop with feet reestablishing support before moving off. Require a visible loading/unweighting-to-support sequence; a pause, wide stance, or ordinary walking step is insufficient. A large jump or perfectly simultaneous landing is not required. Interval: visible preparation/unweighting through renewed support. Key moment: support re-established, if observed.
- chasse step | 并步: A lead foot moves in the travel direction and the trailing foot follows/closes the gap, without passing or crossing the lead foot. Require at least one discernible lead-and-follow cycle. The feet need not touch, and travel may be forward, backward, sideways, or diagonal. Group continuous same-direction cycles; report the count only if observable. Crossing feet supports crossover step instead; sideways body displacement with hidden feet supports movement. Interval: first step initiating the cycle through the final follow step or transition into another pattern. Key moments: none required.
- crossover step | 交叉步: During travel one foot visibly passes across the other foot's path in front or behind. Require a discernible crossing sequence rather than a single perspective-overlapped image. State direction and front/behind crossing only if visible. Interval: initiation of the crossing step through establishment of the next support. If crossing is unclear, use movement. Key moments: none required.
- lunge | 弓步／跨步上网: A reaching step with the lead knee flexing and bearing weight as the body or racket hand reaches toward the shuttle area. Require step-and-load evidence; a stationary squat or torso bend is insufficient. Do not require an ideal textbook posture. Interval: reaching step onset through loaded reach, ending when unloading/push-back begins. A stroke may overlap but gets its own supported event. Key moment: maximum loaded reach, only if visible.
- recovery | 回位: Movement or stance reestablishment after a preceding reach/stroke toward an observable ready position. Do not assume that position is the geometric centre of the court. If chasse step or another named pattern describes these same steps, record that pattern once and write purpose=recovery in Detail; do not duplicate its interval as recovery. Use recovery when the purpose is clear but the specific footwork is not. Interval: exit from the preceding action through the established ready stance or a new action. Key moment: ready stance re-established, if visible.
- scissor jump | 剪刀步起跳／剪刀跳: A rear-court jump with visible body rotation and an exchange of the legs' forward/backward positions. A generic hop is insufficient. Interval: takeoff through landing. Key moments: takeoff and initial landing, if observed. The accompanying stroke is a separate overlapping event.

Stroke labels
- lift | 挑球: A low/underarm stroke from the forecourt whose outgoing shuttle path supports an upward, deep return toward the opponent's rear court. Require the forecourt context and a stroke linked to a discriminative outgoing path; do not require the shuttle's eventual landing or exact contact frame to be visible. Both high defensive and flatter attacking lifts are possible. A raised racket alone or a short rising path without depth evidence is insufficient. Use lift when this family is supported but the forehand/backhand side is unclear. Interval: identifiable striking preparation through follow-through. Key moment: racket-shuttle contact, if localizable.
- forehand lift | 正手挑球: All lift evidence above, plus visible forehand-side striking mechanics relative to the racket arm/body. Do not derive forehand from screen right, the actor's assumed handedness, or the transcript alone. Use lift if the stroke side is unresolved. Same interval/key moment as lift.
- backhand lift | 反手挑球: All lift evidence above, plus visible backhand-side striking mechanics relative to the racket arm/body. Screen left alone does not establish backhand. Do not require a detailed grip view if the visible mechanics otherwise establish the side. Use lift if the side is unresolved. Same interval/key moment as lift.
- net shot | 网前球／放网: A forecourt stroke whose shuttle passes close over the tape and descends short toward the opponent's forecourt. Distinguish from lift by the outgoing trajectory, not just low contact or a gentle swing. Landing need not be visible if the near-net short path is established. Do not claim a spinning/tumbling net shot unless shuttle behaviour supports it.
- drive | 平抽球: A comparatively fast, flat return with an outgoing nearly level trajectory, typically through the midcourt. Require path evidence; swing speed, racket height, and a loud hit alone are insufficient.
- clear | 后场高远球: A stroke from the rear court sending the shuttle deep toward the opponent's rear court. Distinguish it from forecourt lift by the observed contact region and striking context; do not call every high return a clear.
- drop shot | 吊球: A rear-court stroke whose outgoing path descends toward the opponent's forecourt. Distinguish from net shot by the rear-court striking context. Neither a soft-looking swing nor a later low shuttle position alone establishes this label.
- smash | 杀球: A stroke from a high contact region with a visibly steep, fast downward outgoing path. A high racket, jump, or impact sound alone is insufficient. Describe an observed jump as an attribute; do not invent a separate jump-smash label.
- backhand serve | 反手发球: A backhand serving motion that visibly initiates play. Require start-of-play context; a low backhand stroke in a continuing exchange is not enough.
- forehand high serve | 正手发高远球: An observable underarm forehand serving motion that initiates play with a high, deep outgoing trajectory. Require underarm serving mechanics, serving context and the high/deep path. Overhead practice deliveries are not forehand high serves; use racket feed only if training-delivery context is actually visible, otherwise a supported fallback.
For net shot, drive, clear, drop shot, smash, and serves, annotate identifiable striking preparation through follow-through. Their applicable key moment is racket-shuttle contact; mark it uncertain if it cannot be localized.

Training delivery labels (task-specific, separate from serves)
- racket feed | 球拍喂球: A racket strike delivering a shuttle for an observable practice repetition, supported by the visible delivery sequence and repeated drill context. Do not infer this label merely because a rally stops. An overhead feed is not a forehand high serve. Interval: visible delivery preparation through follow-through; key moment racket-shuttle contact if observed.
- hand feed | 手抛喂球: A shuttle is visibly released or tossed from a bare hand to supply the other player. A racket held in the other hand does not make it a stroke. Require hand-release and outgoing shuttle evidence; a raised empty hand alone is insufficient. Interval: hand-delivery preparation through release/follow-through; key moment shuttle release if observed.

Fallback labels (valid annotations, not failures)
- movement | 移动（步法未定）: Body displacement is observable but the foot sequence is not identifiable. State visible travel direction and the missing cue. Do not guess footwork from the movement's destination.
- upward return | 向上回球（球种未定）: A stroke and an outgoing rising shuttle are supported, but forecourt origin or deep trajectory evidence is insufficient for lift. It is more specific than return, but not equivalent to lift.
- return | 回球（球种未定）: Evidence supports striking/redirecting the shuttle but not a more specific stroke label. Exact contact need not occur in a sampled frame: a tracked incoming/outgoing shuttle change linked to the racket motion can support a stroke. Sound alone cannot establish who struck it.
- racket swing | 挥拍（触球未确认）: Racket motion is visible but evidence of shuttle contact/redirection is insufficient. Do not claim an outgoing shot or invent a contact timestamp.


Decision sequence:
- Stroke subtype supported -> use the most specific subtype once. Only rising return supported -> upward return. Stroke supported but subtype unknown -> return. Only racket motion supported -> racket swing.
- Foot sequence supported -> split step, chasse step, crossover step, lunge, scissor jump, or recovery as defined above. Foot sequence unavailable -> movement only if displacement is observed.
- The final report preserves visual evidence and uncertainties. Audio/person identity and sound/action correspondence remain unverified unless a separate alignment stage establishes them; do not silently assign them during merging.

VISUAL REVIEW STAGE. Inspect the actual timestamped frames independently, then correct the candidate JSON. Candidate annotations are hypotheses, not evidence. Return the SAME complete visual-stage JSON schema (shot, actors, storyline, visible_text, actions, uncertainties), with all retained and revised observations; no Markdown or explanation outside JSON.

Focus on these systematic error modes:
1. Drill delivery is not automatically a serve. A forehand high serve has observable underarm serving mechanics as well as start-of-play and high/deep trajectory evidence. An overhead delivery cannot be called forehand high serve merely because an exchange restarts. Use racket feed only when visible repeated-drill delivery or self-fed preparation supports it; otherwise use return/racket swing as supported. Use hand feed for an observed shuttle release from a bare hand, independently of a racket held in the other hand.
2. Check the feet before and after EACH reach. Look for a lead-foot step and trailing-foot follow without crossing (chasse step), a loaded reaching step (lunge), and subsequent recovery. Do not force chasse when the cycle is not visible. Conversely, a wide stance does not by itself prove lunge; cite the reaching step and lead-leg loading. Do not duplicate named footwork as recovery for the same steps.
3. Separate directly observed outgoing shuttle direction from inferred depth. Upward motion from near the net alone does not prove a deep return: lift needs a discriminative outgoing deep path. Landing may be off-screen, but generic steep upward flight must not be asserted to land deep. Choose upward return if the lift subtype lacks depth evidence. Forehand/backhand requires the actor's visible striking mechanics, not a screen-side shortcut.
4. Keep evidence language consistent with certainty. Do not write that deep trajectory is inferred in uncertainties while asserting that it is observed in actions. Do not invent pixel coordinates. Cite actual supplied frame times and directly visible body/racket/shuttle changes. Preserve an unsupported exact contact as null.
5. Include separately supported deliveries and strikes for every person, including a waiting person who later hand-feeds. Do not count gestures without a shuttle release as hand feeds. Keep actions within the caller shot bounds, reference valid shot-scoped actors, and use canonical labels exactly.
6. Correct storyline and actors when correcting actions, so they agree. Preserve text actually readable in these frames. No speech, impact sound, cross-shot identity, deception, or outcome claims can be established by this visual stage alone.
