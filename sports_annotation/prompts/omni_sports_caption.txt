Provide a detailed description of the video, focusing on the fine-grained sports actions of its one or two principal participants and the temporal relationships between those actions and audible events. Produce candidate annotations for human review.

Make sure your description covers the following dimensions:

Visual
- Subjects and characters: distinguish the principal participants by their visible appearance, clothing, position and equipment. Use stable identifiers within each shot, such as S01-P01 and S01-P02. Do not assume identity continuity across cuts. If a shot contains more than two principal participants, report this instead of omitting someone.
- Actions and events: describe the observed behavior chronologically and decompose it into independently distinguishable atomic actions. An atomic action is a meaningful phase with a coherent movement pattern, not necessarily a whole sports attempt and not every small joint movement. Use a supported professional term and describe the visible mechanics in ordinary language.
- Action details: explain how the participant's body and relevant limbs move, how posture and support change, and how equipment or other relevant objects move. Describe the action's progression, not only its final pose. Include contact, release or object flight only when supported by the images.
- Continuous behavior coverage: for each participant in each shot, the annotations should continuously account for the visible behavior. Inspect the behavior before, between and after neighboring actions so that an observable transition is not omitted simply because its endpoints are labeled. Numerical adjacency alone does not establish completeness. Distinguish motion, maintained posture and explanatory gesture. Simultaneous actions may overlap; do not force them into a serial sequence or invent motion to fill stillness, occlusion or cuts.
- Setting and spatial relations: include the environment, relevant objects, participant count and spatial layout needed to understand the actions. For any left/right claim, explicitly distinguish screen coordinates from the participant's anatomical sides. Check movement direction across frames; do not infer travel from a single pose or equipment position. Omit an unsupported direction and state the uncertainty.
- Camera and visibility: note cuts, viewpoint changes, camera movement or occlusion when they affect action interpretation. Do not mistake camera motion for participant motion.
- On-screen text is outside this task. Do not transcribe subtitles, titles, logos or UI, and do not use them as evidence for an action.

Audio
- Speech activity: identify when speech is audible and describe its observable acoustic character when useful. Do not transcribe, translate, summarize or interpret the spoken content. Do not infer which visible person is speaking.
- Non-speech sounds: describe distinguishable transient sounds, sustained sounds, music, ambient/background sounds and non-speech vocalizations using their audible properties. Do not guess a sound's physical source from a plausible sports action.
- Independent segmentation: estimate each audio event's onset and offset from the audio itself. Allow overlapping sounds. Keep an uninterrupted audio event intact across shot cuts or changes of visual action. Do not split it just to create one audio segment per action. Distinguish foreground events from sustained background sound or music.
- If no speech or no audio is audible, state that explicitly. Do not invent events for silent intervals. Subtitles and verbatim transcription are handled outside this task.

Audio-visual correspondence
- Describe temporal overlap between independently identified visual actions and audio events, using their IDs and timestamps.
- Preserve many-to-many relationships: one audio event may overlap multiple actions, including actions in different shots or by different participants; one action may overlap multiple audio events.
- Temporal overlap means only that events occur at the same time. It does not establish who speaks, what speech describes, whether a sound is produced by an action, or any causal relationship.
- Do not adjust either event's boundaries to make audio and motion agree. Preserve separate visual and audio intervals and mark uncertain boundary cases.

It should explicitly include four sections:

1. Storyline: a chronological, natural-language account of the visible behavior and concurrent audible events. Briefly identify the participants and relevant setting. Keep the account consistent with the independently timed events below; a storyline paragraph is not the annotation unit for an atomic action.
2. Atomic Actions: a separate timestamped entry for each distinguishable action, maintained posture or gesture. Include an event ID, participant ID, type, supported technique term and a detailed description of its mechanics. Separate repeated instances. A whole attempt may organize multiple actions, but must not be counted again as an additional atomic action.
3. Audio Events: independently timed speech activity, non-speech vocalizations, other foreground sounds, background sound and music, without transcripts or visible-speaker assignments. Give each event a stable ID, category, foreground/background layer and acoustic description.
4. Temporal Correspondence: for each visual event, list all audio event IDs whose estimated intervals overlap it, with the overlap interval for each pair. Include overlapping background events as background context. Use "none" when there is no overlap; describe uncertain cases instead of forcing a match.
